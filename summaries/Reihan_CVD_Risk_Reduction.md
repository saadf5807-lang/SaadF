### 2.3 10-Year ASCVD Risk Categories (Slide 10)
Estimates the risk of **MI or stroke over 10 years**. Used for ages ⭐ **40–75**. Calculated with the AHA/ACC 2013 pooled cohort calculator (MDCalc).

| Category | 10-y risk |
|---|---|
| **Low** | **< 5%** |
| **Borderline** | **5–7.4%** |
| **Intermediate** | **7.5–19.9%** |
| **High** | **≥ 20%** |

*Mnemonic [added]:* **"5 – 7.5 – 20"**, the three cut-offs.

> 🔁 **Recap: 10-y ASCVD.** Ages 40–75. Low <5, borderline 5–7.4, intermediate 7.5–19.9, high ≥20%.
> 🔗 **Link:** Whatever the risk category, **lifestyle is the foundation for everyone**. Drugs are added on top based on risk. The lecture therefore starts treatment with lifestyle, beginning with diet.

### 2.4 Lifestyle: Diet (Slides 11–15)
⭐ **Recommended intakes (Slide 12):**
| Component | Target |
|---|---|
| **Saturated fat** | **< 10%** of total energy, replaced by **polyunsaturated** fat |
| **Trans fat** | **< 1%** of total energy |
| **Salt** | **< 5 g/day** |
| **Fibre** | **30–45 g/day** (wholegrain, fruit, vegetables) |
| **Fruit** | **200 g/day** (2–3 servings) |
| **Vegetables** | **200 g/day** (2–3 servings) |
| **Fish** | **≥ 2×/week, one oily** |

- ⭐ **Mediterranean diet**: observational benefit for CV risk reduction. **PREDIMED** (NEJM 2018): primary prevention with a Mediterranean diet supplemented with **extra-virgin olive oil or nuts**.
- Nutritional counseling: food pyramid (base = daily exercise, portion & weight control, then vegetables/fruit/whole grains/healthy oils; top = limit **red meat, butter, sugary drinks, sweets, salt**).
- ⭐ **Tailored dietary intervention** combines 3 inputs:
  1. **Individual factors**: genetics, sex, age, BMI, physical activity (job/leisure), cultural habits
  2. **CV risk factors & comorbidities**: HTN, DM, dyslipidemia, CKD
  3. **CV disease**: IHD, HF

*Mnemonic [added]:* the **"<10, <1, <5"** rule: sat fat <10%, trans fat <1%, salt <5 g.

> 🔁 **Recap: Diet.** <10% saturated, <1% trans, <5 g salt, 30–45 g fibre, 200 g fruit + 200 g veg, fish twice weekly (one oily). Mediterranean diet (PREDIMED). Tailor to the individual, their risk factors, and their disease.
> 🔗 **Link:** Diet is energy in. Physical activity is energy out, and the second lifestyle pillar.
> ▶️ **Up next: Physical activity.** Prescription, session structure, Saudi data.

### 2.5 Lifestyle: Physical Activity (Slides 16–19)
- Aerobic exercise should be offered to patients with **known CAD**
- ⭐ **Stable angina or stable chronic HF**: **moderate-to-vigorous** aerobic training **≥ 3×/week, 30 min/session**
- **Sedentary** patients: strongly encourage starting **light-intensity** programs

⭐ **Typical exercise session:**
1. **Warm-up: 5–10 min**
2. **Aerobic conditioning: up to 30 min or more** (walking, jogging, cycling, stairs)
3. **Cool-down: 5–7 min**

**KSA physical-activity insufficiency (MOH World Health Survey):**
| Highest insufficiency | Lowest insufficiency |
|---|---|
| **Hail 91%**, **Madinah 90%**, **Qasim 88%** | **Tabuk 64%**, **Bahah 65%** |

⭐ **Take-home:**
- Lifestyle improvement should **begin early** and underlie **all** preventive efforts
- **Most CV events are preventable** with lifestyle modification
- ⭐ **"Some is better than none; more is even better"**

> 🔁 **Recap: Physical activity.** ≥3×/week × 30 min moderate–vigorous for stable CAD/HF. Session = warm-up 5–10, aerobic ~30, cool-down 5–7. KSA inactivity is highest in Hail (91%). Some beats none.
> 🔗 **Link:** Diet and exercise are the things to add. The next lifestyle change is removing something, and it is the single most powerful one: smoking.
> ▶️ **Up next: Tobacco cessation.** Mortality benefit, time course, and pharmacotherapy safety in CAD.

### 2.6 Lifestyle: Tobacco Cessation (Slides 20–22)
- ⭐⭐ **Smoking cessation = the MOST important and MOST cost-effective** lifestyle modification for preventing CVD
- In patients with prior **MI, CABG, angioplasty, or known CHD**, quitting reduces mortality: **RR 0.64 (CI 0.58–0.71)**, i.e., ⭐ **36% ↓ mortality after MI**
- ⭐ **Time course:** mortality risk **↓ 50% in the first couple of years**. It **approaches non-smokers' risk in ~5–15 years** (Critchley & Capewell, 2012).
- Eliminating health-risk behaviours could prevent **≥ 80% of CVD** and **40% of cancers** (BACPR, 2017)
- *(Slide 21 "If You Quit Smoking Right Now" has blank signposts in the PDF, so its content was not captured.)*

⭐ **Pharmacotherapy in CAD:**
| Agent | Safety in CAD |
|---|---|
| **Nicotine replacement therapy** | ⭐ **Safe**, should be **routinely offered** |
| **Bupropion** | Safe in stable CAD (some studies) |
| **Varenicline** | Safe in some studies, **but a meta-analysis found a small, statistically significant ↑ CVD** |

> 🔁 **Recap: Smoking.** Most important and most cost-effective. RR 0.64 = 36% ↓ mortality post-MI. Risk halves in ~2 years and approaches a non-smoker's in 5–15 years. NRT is safe. Varenicline has a CVD signal.
> 🔗 **Link:** That completes lifestyle. The lecture now turns to the modifiable disease risk factors that need targets and often drugs, starting with the #1 CV risk factor worldwide.

### 2.8 Hyperlipidemia (Slides 25–30)
- ⭐ One of the **most important (and modifiable)** CAD risk factors → **accelerated atherosclerosis**
- KSA: **43%** of survey respondents have **raised serum cholesterol**

**Risk factors (Slide 26):** diet, age, DM, inactive lifestyle, family history, gender, medications, genetic mutations

⭐ **Diagnosis (Slide 27):**
- **Fasting or non-fasting** lipid profile (TC, HDL, TG)
- ⭐ Pathological values on **2 separate occasions** are needed to confirm
- ⭐ **Dyslipidemia = LDL > 130 mg/dL and/or HDL < 40 mg/dL**

| Parameter | Optimal | Pathological |
|---|---|---|
| **Total cholesterol** | **200 mg/dL** (i.e., < 200) | Borderline **200–239**; High **> 240** |
| **TG** | **150 mg/dL** (i.e., < 150) | Borderline **150–199**; High **> 200**; Very high **≥ 500** |
| **LDL** | **100 mg/dL** (i.e., < 100) | Near-optimal **100–129**; Borderline high **> 130**; High **> 160**; Very high **≥ 190** |
| **HDL** | **≥ 60 mg/dL** | Low **< 40** |
| **LDL/HDL ratio** | ♂ **< 3.5**; ♀ **< 3.0** | ♂ **> 3.5**; ♀ **> 3.0** |

**Management (Slide 28):**
- Non-pharmacological: dietary changes, **↓ saturated fat & cholesterol**, **eliminate alcohol**, healthy weight, physical activity
- Medical: ⭐ **Statins** (first line) → **PCSK9 inhibitors** (second line if poor statin response)

⭐⭐⭐ **Who gets a statin? (Slide 29).** The **4 statin benefit groups** *[the term "benefit groups" is added]*:
| # | Group |
|---|---|
| 1 | **Clinical ASCVD** |
| 2 | **LDL ≥ 190 mg/dL**, age **≥ 21** |
| 3 | **Diabetics**, **LDL 70–189**, age **40–75** |
| 4 | **10-y ASCVD risk ≥ 20%** |
| (+) | **ASCVD 7.5–20% + risk enhancers**: family history of **premature** ASCVD · **metabolic syndrome** · **CKD** · **inflammatory conditions** · **elevated biomarkers** (lipoproteins, **hs-CRP**) |

*Mnemonic [added]:* **"A-190-DM-20"**: **A**SCVD, LDL **190**, **DM** 40–75, risk **20**%.

⭐ **Take-home (Slide 30):**
- **First assess ASCVD risk**
- Statins + lifestyle in higher-risk patients; **maximally tolerated dose** for the highest risk (clinical ASCVD)
- **PCSK9i reduce events when added to statins** in **very-high-risk secondary-prevention** patients
- ⭐ **"Lower is better"**

> 🔁 **Recap: Hyperlipidemia.** Diagnose on 2 occasions (LDL >130 and/or HDL <40). Statin groups: ASCVD, LDL ≥190, DM 40–75 with LDL 70–189, risk ≥20% (or 7.5–20% + enhancers). Statin first, then PCSK9i. Lower is better.
> 🔗 **Link:** Diabetes appeared inside the statin criteria, and it is itself a major CV risk multiplier. The lecture now asks how treating DM itself can protect the heart.
> ▶️ **Up next: Diabetes mellitus.** CV risk, and the cardioprotective drug classes (SGLT-2i, GLP-1 RA).

### 2.9 Diabetes Mellitus (Slides 31–33)
- ⭐ DM → **2–3× ↑ CVD risk**
- **CVD is a more likely cause of hospitalization** in diabetics than diabetes-related causes
- ⭐ Experts recommend **prevention of CV events as the goal** of diabetes treatment
- ⭐ **SGLT-2 inhibitors** and **GLP-1 receptor agonists** showed **CV protection** in outcome trials (T2DM with high CV risk)
- ⭐ Consider them when **ASCVD, HF, or CKD predominates, independent of HbA1c**
- **Dapagliflozin** (SGLT-2i) is added if **metformin monotherapy** fails to reach **HbA1c < 7%**. In the lecture's case (HbA1c **6.2%** on metformin, no ASCVD/HF/CKD) it is **not needed**.
- ADA: **lifestyle management + medications**, including nutrition therapy, physical activity, smoking cessation

> 🔁 **Recap: DM.** 2–3× CVD risk. The goal of treatment is preventing CV events. SGLT-2i/GLP-1 RA for ASCVD, HF, or CKD regardless of HbA1c. Otherwise add them only if HbA1c is ≥7% on metformin.
> 🔗 **Link:** The last preventive tool is the most commonly misused: aspirin. Unlike statins, its benefit is small and it carries a bleeding risk, so the rules are restrictive.
> ▶️ **Up next: Aspirin for primary prevention (USPSTF 2023).**

## 4. Rules, Exceptions, Patterns
- **Lifestyle for everyone, first and always.** Drugs are added according to risk.
- **The 10-y ASCVD calculator is for ages 40–75.** Exceptions: **LDL ≥190 (age ≥21)** and **clinical ASCVD** get statins **without** calculating risk.
- **DM aged 40–75 with LDL 70–189 → statin**, even if 10-y risk is low.
- **Intermediate risk (7.5–20%) → statin only with risk enhancers.**
- **Dyslipidemia needs 2 abnormal measurements**, fasting or non-fasting.
- **Aspirin for primary prevention is the exception, not the rule.** Consider only at 40–59 with ≥10% risk and low bleeding risk. **Never start at ≥60.**
- **SGLT-2i/GLP-1 RA:** the indication in ASCVD, HF, or CKD is **independent of HbA1c**. Otherwise escalate only if HbA1c ≥7% on metformin.
- **Smoking cessation is the #1 lifestyle measure; HTN is the #1 risk factor.** Don't mix them up.
- **NRT is safe in CAD.** Varenicline has a small CVD signal (meta-analysis).
- **Lipids: "lower is better."**

## 5. Clinical Correlations
- **Post-MI smoker**: quitting gives a 36% mortality reduction, larger than many drugs *[comparison added]*. Offer NRT routinely.
- **Diabetic with established ASCVD/HF/CKD**: add SGLT-2i/GLP-1 RA even if HbA1c is at target.
- **Patient with LDL ≥190** (e.g., familial hypercholesterolemia *[added]*): high-intensity statin from age 21, no calculator needed.
- **Elderly patient asking to start aspirin "for the heart"**: USPSTF D, do not start (bleeding outweighs benefit). *[added: this concerns primary prevention only. Aspirin in established CAD (secondary prevention) remains indicated, per the CAD lecture.]*
- **Stable CAD/HF patient**: prescribe structured aerobic exercise (≥3×/wk, 30 min) as cardiac rehabilitation.
- **KSA context**: high inactivity (≥88% in Hail/Madinah/Qasim) and 43% raised cholesterol make population-level lifestyle measures the priority.

## 8. Exam Pitfalls & Traps
1. **"#1 risk factor" (HTN) vs "most important/cost-effective intervention" (smoking cessation).** These are different questions.
2. **Aspirin for everyone at risk.** No: 40–59 with ≥10% risk only (C). **≥60 is D.** Low-risk patients: avoid.
3. **Applying the 10-y calculator outside ages 40–75**, or to patients with LDL ≥190 or ASCVD (they get statins regardless).
4. **Forgetting the DM statin rule.** A diabetic aged 40–75 with LDL 70–189 needs a statin even if the 10-y risk is under 7.5%.
5. **Intermediate risk ≠ automatic statin.** Risk enhancers are required.
6. **Adding an SGLT-2i because the patient "has diabetes."** It is needed only with ASCVD/HF/CKD or HbA1c ≥7% on metformin.
7. **Diagnosing dyslipidemia from a single value.** Two occasions are required. Fasting is **not** mandatory.
8. **Premature family history** means a **first-degree** relative (♂ <55, ♀ <65, per the CAD lecture). Aunts, uncles and grandparents don't count.
9. **Varenicline "totally safe" in CAD.** The meta-analysis showed a small ↑ CVD. **NRT is the safe routine choice.**
10. **Confusing risk bands:** **7.5%** (statin threshold with enhancers) vs **10%** (aspirin threshold) vs **20%** (high risk, statin).
11. **Time course of smoking benefit:** 50% ↓ within ~2 years, but **5–15 years** to reach non-smoker levels. It is not immediate.
